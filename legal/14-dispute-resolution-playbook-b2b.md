## INTRODUCTION

This Playbook provides guidance for B2B (business-to-business) dispute scenarios on AllSquared. B2B disputes differ from consumer disputes in that:
- Both parties are businesses operating in a commercial capacity
- ADR is voluntary (applies only if both parties agreed in writing in the SOW or MSA)
- Disputes often involve larger values and more complex contract interpretation
- Parties are more likely to be represented by solicitors
- Evidentiary standards are higher (less consumer protection, more commercial realism)

This Playbook covers 10 B2B scenarios common on freelance platforms. Each scenario follows the same structure as the Consumer Playbook.

## SCENARIO 1: SOW MILESTONE SIGN-OFF DISPUTE — ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA AMBIGUOUS OR MISSING

### Overview

**Common situation:** Freelancer has delivered a Milestone but the SOW's acceptance criteria are ambiguous or missing. Client refuses to click "Accept" on the Platform, withholding payment pending clarification. Freelancer argues the work meets the (unclear) criteria and demands payment.

### Trigger

- Dispute Window initiated by one party
- Freelancer claims: "Work is complete and meets the SOW"
- Client claims: "Acceptance criteria are unclear; I cannot accept without clarification"

### Triage

**Urgency:** High  
**Category:** Contract Interpretation / Acceptance Criteria  
**Risk Level:** High (payment and relationship at risk)

**Initial Assessment:**
1. Does the SOW specify acceptance criteria? (Detailed or vague?)
2. Are the criteria objective (measurable) or subjective?
3. Has Client provided specific defects or are they just withholding acceptance without justification?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Request SOW Acceptance Criteria section | Day 1–2 |
| AllSquared compares SOW to delivered work | Day 5–10 |
| Tier 1 AI assessment | Day 15 |
| Tier 2 ADR Official determination | **Day 45–90** |

### Evidence Required

**From Both Parties:**
- The signed SOW with Acceptance Criteria section
- Detailed Deliverables table showing criteria
- Actual delivered work/files
- Emails exchanging about acceptance criteria during the engagement

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **Are the acceptance criteria objective and measurable?** If yes, easier to assess. If no, harder.

2. **Does the delivered work meet the stated criteria?** — Clause 5.4 of the SOW template should specify "Acceptance Criteria" column.

3. **Is ambiguity a Client drafting failure?** — If the Client's SOW is vague, favour the Freelancer (Freelancer met the contract as written).

4. **Did Client provide feedback during the engagement for the Freelancer to cure?** — If yes, Freelancer had opportunity to fix. If no, Client's silence may mean acceptance.

5. **Is Client withholding acceptance for bad faith?** — Example: Client got the work, liked it, but wants to avoid payment. That is bad faith withholding.

### Possible Outcomes

**Favour Freelancer (Full Payment):**
- Acceptance criteria are clear and work meets them
- Client's withholding is without justification
- Freelancer gets full payment release
- Client may be sanctioned for bad faith

**Favour Client (Partial Payment + Rework):**
- Criteria are partially met, but gaps exist
- Freelancer must rework within 10 days
- Pro-rata payment released pending rework

**Clarification + Revised Acceptance:**
- SOW criteria are so ambiguous that neither party is at fault
- ADR Official provides interpretation of the criteria
- Freelancer delivers revised work to meet clarified criteria
- Payment released on revised acceptance

**Cost Sanction Against Client:**
- B2B ADR fee (if applicable) shifted to Client for frivolous withholding
- Client ordered to pay additional penalty (professional conduct sanction)

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Escalate if:**
- Relationship is ongoing and parties want to salvage it through mediation
- Root cause is poor communication, not contract breach

## SCENARIO 2: CHANGE REQUEST DISPUTE — CLIENT CLAIMS IN-SCOPE; FREELANCER CLAIMS CHANGE REQUEST REQUIRED

### Overview

**Common situation:** Client requests modifications to the Deliverables (additional features, format changes, scope expansion). Client claims the change is "in-scope" and should be free. Freelancer claims it is "out-of-scope" and requires a formal Change Request with additional fee.

### Trigger

- Client makes a request (email, Slack, Platform message)
- Freelancer responds: "That's out of scope. Submit a Change Request."
- Client refuses, claims it's in-scope
- Dispute arises; Client threatens to withhold payment

### Triage

**Urgency:** Medium  
**Category:** Scope / Change Management  
**Risk Level:** Medium (contract interpretation; both parties may have reasonable views)

**Initial Assessment:**
1. What is the requested change?
2. Does it fall within the SOW Scope of Work or the "Out of Scope" section?
3. Is the Scope description detailed enough to make a determination?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Request SOW Scope section | Day 1–2 |
| Compare requested change to Scope | Day 3–5 |
| Tier 1 AI assessment | Day 15 |
| Tier 2 ADR Official determination | **Day 30–60** |

### Evidence Required

**From Both Parties:**
- Signed SOW with Scope of Work section and Out of Scope list
- Client's request (email, message, description of change)
- Freelancer's response (why they view it as out-of-scope)
- SOW Deliverables table showing what was to be delivered

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **What does the SOW Scope section say?** — Is the requested change explicitly included or excluded?

2. **Is the requested change a natural extension of in-scope work?** — Example:
   - SOW: "Design a logo" → Client asks: "Can you add a version with different colors?" (natural extension, likely in-scope)
   - SOW: "Write blog post" → Client asks: "Can you also do SEO optimization?" (separate service, likely out-of-scope)

3. **Did Client's request represent a material additional effort?** — If minimal (5% additional work), may be absorbed in-scope. If significant (50%), out-of-scope.

4. **What did the Freelancer's estimate assume?** — Review the SOW budget/timeline. Does it account for the requested change?

5. **Did Client approve a Change Request if one was submitted?** — Check MSA Clause 4.4 on Change Request procedure.

### Possible Outcomes

**Change Is In-Scope (Freelancer Must Comply):**
- Requested change falls within SOW definition
- Freelancer must include it without additional fee
- Freelancer delivers within current timeline (or extends without cost)

**Change Is Out-of-Scope (Client Must Pay):**
- Requested change is not covered by SOW
- Freelancer is correct to require a Change Request and additional fee
- Client may accept Change Request and pay, or decline the change

**Partial In/Out (Blended Outcome):**
- Part of the request is in-scope (included in payment)
- Part is out-of-scope (requires Change Request fee)
- Pro-rata cost estimated

**Revised SOW with Change Request:**
- Dispute arises because original SOW was vague
- ADR Official clarifies scope and orders a Change Request be issued
- Parties negotiate Change Request terms
- Freelancer proceeds with work once Change Request is agreed

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Escalate if:**
- Relationship is ongoing and parties want to establish clear change management process going forward

## SCENARIO 3: IP ASSIGNMENT COMPLETION DISPUTE — FREELANCER CONDITIONS IP ASSIGNMENT ON FULL PAYMENT; PAYMENT IN DISPUTE

### Overview

**Common situation:** Freelancer has delivered work and Client has accepted it, but disputes payment. Freelancer refuses to assign IP until full payment is received. Client claims IP should be assigned immediately upon delivery; withholding assignment is a breach.

### Trigger

- Client accepts Deliverable on Platform (triggers Auto-Release of Escrow)
- But Client is disputing payment for a prior Milestone
- Freelancer conditions IP assignment on receipt of payment
- Client says: "The IP is already mine; you must assign it"

### Triage

**Urgency:** High  
**Category:** IP / Payment Condition  
**Risk Level:** High (multiple disputes intertwined)

**Initial Assessment:**
1. What is the payment dispute (quality issue? Scope dispute?)?
2. Does Clause 6.1 require assignment "upon full payment"?
3. Has Client actually received full payment or is Client in breach?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Clarify payment status (has Client's payment cleared to Freelancer?) | Day 1 |
| Determine if Client is disputing payment legitimately or avoiding it | Day 3 |
| Resolve underlying payment dispute first | Day 10–30 |
| Then address IP assignment as follow-on remedy | Day 30–45 |

### Evidence Required

**From Freelancer:**
- MSA Clause 6.1 language: "Assignment on full payment"
- Evidence of non-payment or payment dispute
- IP assignment document (prepared and ready to sign once payment cleared)

**From Client:**
- Evidence of payment sent (bank transfer, Escrow release confirmation)
- If withholding payment, justification (quality issue, non-delivery)

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **Does Clause 6.1 condition assignment on "full payment"?** — Check exact language. MSA Template Clause 6.1 says: "Upon full payment of fees and satisfaction of all conditions for Escrow release, all Foreground IP…is assigned to the Client with full title guarantee."

2. **Has full payment been made?** — Check Escrow account:
   - Has Client's deposit reached Freelancer? (Yes = full payment)
   - Or is Client disputing the obligation to pay? (Yes = payment dispute must be resolved first)

3. **Is Freelancer's withholding of assignment reasonable?** — Yes, if:
   - Payment is genuinely not received
   - Clause 6.1 clearly makes assignment conditional on payment
   - This is standard contract practice (no payment, no IP transfer)

4. **Or is assignment a separate obligation?** — Some might argue:
   - Acceptance triggers assignment automatically (Client accepted Deliverable)
   - IP assignment cannot be withheld for payment disputes (separate remedies)
   — This is a harder argument; Clause 6.1 is explicit about the condition

### Possible Outcomes

**Payment Dispute Resolved First:**
- ADR Official determines the underlying payment dispute (is Client obligated to pay?)
- Once payment obligation is clarified, IP assignment follows automatically
- If Client owes payment, Client pays and Freelancer assigns IP
- If Freelancer is in breach, refund is ordered and Client retains IP

**Separability Doctrine (Rare):**
- If payment dispute is frivolous or Client has paid substantially, ADR Official may order:
  - Immediate IP assignment even if payment is in dispute
  - Client's remedy is the payment dispute, not withholding of IP
  — This overrides the "on full payment" condition if Client acted in good faith

**Conditional Assignment:**
- IP assignment is ordered conditional on final resolution of payment dispute
- Escrow holds the IP assignment document until payment is confirmed
- Then assignment is automatically executed

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Escalate if:**
- Complex intertwining of disputes (payment + IP + quality issues) needs holistic resolution
- Parties want to establish a settlement that addresses all disputes together

## SCENARIO 4: CONFIDENTIALITY BREACH CLAIM — ONE PARTY ALLEGES DISCLOSURE OF CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION

### Overview

**Common situation:** Client claims Freelancer disclosed the Client's Confidential Information (trade secrets, customer lists, strategy documents, source code) to third parties or used it for Freelancer's other clients. Freelancer denies or claims the information was not actually Confidential.

### Trigger

- Client discovers (or suspects) that Freelancer has disclosed Confidential Information
- Client claims breach of Clause 7 (Confidentiality) of the MSA
- Freelancer either denies disclosure or claims the information was not marked as "Confidential"

### Triage

**Urgency:** Critical  
**Category:** Breach of Confidence / Data Protection  
**Risk Level:** Critical (reputational harm, potential regulatory issues)

**Immediate Assessment:**
1. What is the alleged disclosure? (to whom, when, what information)
2. Is the information actually marked as "Confidential" in the SOW?
3. Has harm been done? (Customer defection, competitor advantage, etc.)
4. Is this a regulatory matter (GDPR breach) or contractual only?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Immediate preservation of evidence (don't destroy) | Day 1 |
| Demand response from Freelancer | Day 2 |
| Forensic investigation (if needed) | Day 10–20 |
| Tier 1 AI assessment | Day 20 |
| **Tier 2 ADR Official determination** | **Day 45–90** |

### Evidence Required

**From Client:**
- Evidence of the disclosure (email showing Freelancer sent documents to third party, witness statement, etc.)
- Proof that the information was marked "Confidential" (SOW header, document headers, email statements)
- Proof of harm (business impact, customer complaints, competitive disadvantage)
- Copy of the Confidential Information itself (redacted if needed)
- GDPR angle: Does the information include personal data? (If yes, may require data breach notification)

**From Freelancer:**
- Rebuttal of disclosure (evidence that information was not shared, or shared only with authorized parties)
- If sharing occurred, explanation (was the third party under NDA? Was sharing authorized? Was information Public Domain?)
- Argument that information was not "Confidential" (not marked, not treated as confidential, etc.)

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **Is the information actually Confidential?** — Under MSA Clause 7, Confidential Information is:
   - Marked as "Confidential" at the time of disclosure, OR
   - Information that is reasonably understood to be confidential by nature

   If Client did not mark the information as Confidential and did not treat it as confidential (e.g., posted it on Client's public website), harder to claim confidentiality breach.

2. **Did Freelancer disclose the information?** — This requires proof:
   - Email showing disclosure (smoking gun)
   - Witness statement (less reliable)
   - Circumstantial evidence (Freelancer had access, information appeared elsewhere, timing suggests disclosure)

3. **To whom was it disclosed?** — If disclosed to authorized parties (Freelancer's accountant, lawyer, Freelancer's employees under NDA), not a breach. If disclosed to competitors or the public, breach.

4. **Was there authorization?** — Did Client expressly or implicitly authorize the disclosure? (Example: Client told Freelancer, "Feel free to show this to your colleagues to get feedback" = implied authorization)

5. **Is this also a GDPR breach?** — If the Confidential Information contains personal data (names, emails, phone numbers of Client's customers), disclosure may trigger GDPR breach notification obligations.

### Tier 1 AI Focus

- **Evidence Strength:** Is there direct evidence of disclosure or circumstantial?
- **Confidentiality Marking:** Was the information marked as Confidential?
- **Harm Quantification:** What is the business impact of the disclosure?
- **GDPR Flag:** Does the information contain personal data? (If yes, escalate to data protection authority)

### Possible Outcomes

**Breach Found (Freelancer Liable):**
- Clear evidence of unauthorized disclosure
- Information is Confidential under Clause 7
- Freelancer is liable for damages
- Remedies may include:
  - Damages (compensation for harm)
  - Injunction (cease further disclosure)
  - Specific performance (recall information, delete from third party)
  - Account of profits (if Freelancer profited from disclosure)

**Breach Not Found:**
- No credible evidence of disclosure
- Information was not actually Confidential
- Freelancer is not liable
- Client must accept the outcome

**Partial Breach (Some Information, Limited Scope):**
- Freelancer disclosed some but not all of Client's Confidential Information
- Disclosure was to authorized parties only (limited harm)
- Freelancer is liable for limited damages (reduced from full claim)

**Regulatory Referral (GDPR/DPA):**
- If personal data was involved and disclosed without authorization, this is a GDPR breach
- ADR determination should include:
  - Findings on contractual breach (Clause 7)
  - Referral to Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) if UK data controller
  - Recommendation that both parties conduct data protection impact assessment

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Do NOT escalate to CEDR.** Confidentiality breaches are serious and typically require court determination or regulatory investigation, not mediation.

## SCENARIO 5: IR35 / OFF-PAYROLL WORKING RECLASSIFICATION RISK — HOW TO HANDLE MID-ENGAGEMENT

### Overview

**Common situation:** During the engagement, HMRC (or another tax authority) reclassifies the Freelancer's engagement as "off-payroll working" or "disguised employment," requiring Client to deduct income tax and National Insurance. Freelancer claims this is not a Contract breach but a tax matter; Client seeks indemnity or fee reduction.

### Trigger

- HMRC sends Client a notice of reclassification
- Client informs Freelancer that tax withholding is now required
- Freelancer claims the engagement was always genuinely self-employed
- Client seeks to adjust fees or withdraw from engagement
- Dispute arises

### Triage

**Urgency:** High (tax and regulatory implications)  
**Category:** Tax / Employment Status  
**Risk Level:** High (HMRC involvement; potential fines)

**Immediate Assessment:**
1. Has HMRC actually issued a ruling or is this Client speculation?
2. What is the basis of the reclassification (control, integration, mutuality)?
3. What is the Freelancer's actual working arrangement? (Full-time? Integrated into Client team? Using Client's equipment?)

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Obtain HMRC ruling (if issued) or Client's basis for concern | Day 1–5 |
| Request Freelancer's response | Day 5 |
| ADR Official reviews engagement terms against IR35 tests | Day 15 |
| Tier 1 AI assessment | Day 20 |
| **Tier 2 ADR Official determination** | **Day 45–90** |

### Evidence Required

**From Client:**
- HMRC correspondence (or reason for reclassification concern)
- Details of the Freelancer's working arrangement: location (Client's office or remote?), hours (fixed or flexible?), equipment (Client-provided or own?), control (Client supervises or self-directed?)
- Tax consequences (withholding requirements, deadlines, costs)

**From Freelancer:**
- Argument that the engagement is genuinely self-employed
- Evidence supporting self-employment: other clients, own equipment, flexible hours, ability to refuse work, right to substitute, etc.
- Tax advice (if available) on IR35 classification

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **Is this ADR's role or a tax authority matter?** — ADR Official is not a tax expert. This may be beyond ADR scope. Consider:
   - If HMRC has ruled, the ruling is binding; ADR cannot overrule it
   - If no ruling yet, ADR can assess the contractual relationship's IR35 risk

2. **What does the engagement look like?** — IR35 looks at:
   - **Control:** Does Client dictate how work is done? (Self-employed = less control)
   - **Integration:** Is Freelancer integrated into Client's team? (Employee-like = reclassification risk)
   - **Mutuality of Obligation:** Is there obligation to provide work and accept it? (Yes = employment-like)
   - **Personal Service:** Can Freelancer send a substitute? (Yes = self-employed; No = employment-like)
   - **Right to Manage:** Does Freelancer manage own workload and hours? (Yes = self-employed)

3. **Is the MSA and SOW consistent with self-employment?** — Check:
   - Does the MSA say Freelancer is independent contractor? (Yes, MSA Clause 15.10 says so)
   - Can Freelancer work from home/remote? (Yes, likely self-employed)
   - Can Freelancer reject work? (Yes, self-employed; No, employment-like)
   - Is the engagement exclusive or non-exclusive? (Exclusive = reclassification risk; Non-exclusive = self-employed)

4. **If reclassification occurs, who bears the cost?** — This is a matter for Client and HMRC:
   - Client is responsible for deducting tax/NIC if reclassified
   - Freelancer's fee may need adjustment (net vs. gross)
   — But this is a tax matter, not a contract breach

### Possible Outcomes

**Self-Employment Status Confirmed:**
- ADR Official finds the engagement is genuinely self-employed
- No IR35 reclassification required
- If Client is concerned about HMRC challenge, Client should obtain tax advice, not seek fee reduction
- Freelancer continues unchanged

**Reclassification Acknowledged (but outside ADR scope):**
- ADR Official finds the engagement has characteristics of employment
- Reclassification is likely if HMRC reviews it
- But this is a tax/employment matter, not a contract dispute
- ADR declines jurisdiction; parties should seek tax and employment advice
- If Client wants to modify the arrangement going forward, parties should amend the SOW

**Fee Adjustment (Negotiated Outcome):**
- If reclassification is credible, Client's tax costs increase
- Instead of Client unilaterally cutting fees, parties negotiate a new fee that accounts for withholding
- Example: Old fee = £10,000 net. New fee = £12,500 gross (Client withholds tax, Freelancer receives net ~£10,000)
- Parties execute an amendment to the SOW

**Engagement Termination (Mutual):**
- If IR35 exposure is too high for Client and reclassification is credible, Client and Freelancer may agree to terminate
- Pro-rata payment for work to date
- Clean break

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Escalate if:**
- Parties want mediation to negotiate a revised fee arrangement that accounts for tax changes

## SCENARIO 6: POST-TERMINATION OWNERSHIP OF PARTIALLY COMPLETED DELIVERABLES

### Overview

**Common situation:** Engagement is terminated mid-way (either party terminates for convenience or cause). Client claims ownership of partially completed work and wants to hand off to another Freelancer. Freelancer claims ownership of work-in-progress and demands payment for completed portions.

### Trigger

- Either party terminates the engagement (MSA Clause 3.2 or 3.3)
- Deliverables are only partially complete
- Client wants to take the partial work and hire another Freelancer to finish
- Freelancer claims ownership of WIP and demands payment for effort to date

### Triage

**Urgency:** High  
**Category:** Termination / IP Ownership  
**Risk Level:** High (both parties' interests at stake)

**Immediate Assessment:**
1. What is the reason for termination?
2. What % of work is complete?
3. Does the SOW specify what happens to WIP on termination?
4. Has the Client paid deposits for the unfinished portions?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Clarify termination reason and Escrow status | Day 1 |
| Request evidence of work completed | Day 3 |
| Assess value of WIP | Day 5–10 |
| Tier 1 AI assessment | Day 15 |
| **Tier 2 ADR Official determination** | **Day 30–60** |

### Evidence Required

**From Client:**
- Termination notice and reason
- Evidence that Client accepted prior Milestones (these are Client's to keep)
- Description of the partially completed work (what is done, what is missing)
- Proof of advance payment for unfinished work (if any)

**From Freelancer:**
- Evidence of work completed (drafts, notes, time logs)
- Description of what is done and what remains
- Calculation of hours/value of work to date

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **What does the MSA say about WIP on termination?** — Check MSA Clause 3.4:
   "Upon termination:…(e) the Freelancer must grant the Client a licence to use any partially completed Deliverables, unless disputed"

   This suggests Client gets a non-exclusive licence to WIP, but ownership/control may vary.

2. **Who pays for the WIP?** — Two scenarios:
   - Client paid in advance (Escrow deposited) = Client should get WIP delivered
   - Client did not pay = Freelancer may withhold WIP unless termination is Client's breach

3. **Is WIP usable by Client?** — If WIP is incomplete and incoherent, it may be worthless to Client. In that case, Client doesn't get much value even with a licence.

4. **Why was the engagement terminated?**
   - Termination for Client convenience (MSA 3.2) = Client must pay for WIP
   - Termination for Freelancer breach (MSA 3.3) = Client may get WIP without full payment
   - Termination for Force Majeure = WIP allocation is neutral/pro-rata

### Possible Outcomes

**Client Terminates for Convenience (Client pays pro-rata):**
- Client gets ownership or licence to WIP
- Client pays Freelancer for work completed to termination date
- Pro-rata payment calculated based on % of Milestone completed
- Example: Milestone is 50% done when Client terminates for convenience → Client pays 50% of Milestone fee

**Client Terminates for Cause (Freelancer breach):**
- Client gets WIP but may not pay for unfinished portions
- Client pays only for work that meets acceptance criteria
- If WIP is unusable, Client may get only a minimal payment
- Example: Freelancer stops working mid-project; Client terminates → Client pays only for usable completed portions

**Freelancer Terminates (for Client breach or convenience):**
- Freelancer retains ownership of WIP (leverage for payment)
- Client may get a limited licence to use WIP for internal purposes only
- Freelancer is paid for work completed
- Client must either hire replacement Freelancer to continue (using WIP as base) or restart from scratch

**Negotiated Buyout:**
- Client wants WIP and offers Freelancer a "finish fee" (e.g., 25% of remaining work) to transfer WIP
- Parties agree: Client gets full ownership of WIP, Freelancer gets payment for completed work + finish fee
- Freelancer transitions the work and provides training/handoff to replacement

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Escalate if:**
- Relationship can potentially be salvaged and parties want to discuss revised terms to avoid full termination

## SCENARIO 7: SUBCONTRACTOR DISPUTE SURFACING AT MAIN CONTRACT LEVEL — FREELANCER BLAMES UNAPPROVED SUBCONTRACTOR

### Overview

**Common situation:** Client discovers (or suspects) that Freelancer has used a subcontractor to perform the work. Client claims this violates the SOW (which required Freelancer personally to do the work, or required subcontractor pre-approval). Freelancer claims the subcontractor was approved or that subcontracting is standard practice.

### Trigger

- Client discovers the Deliverable was actually produced by a Freelancer's subcontractor
- Freelancer did not disclose the subcontracting or seek approval
- Client feels deceived: "I hired you, not someone else"
- Dispute arises; Client may refuse payment or seek damages

### Triage

**Urgency:** Medium  
**Category:** Performance / Subcontracting  
**Risk Level:** Medium (depends on whether subcontracting was actually unauthorized)

**Initial Assessment:**
1. Does the SOW or MSA require Freelancer to personally do the work, or permit subcontracting?
2. Was a subcontractor actually used?
3. If yes, was it approved by Client or disclosed?
4. Is the quality of the subcontractor's work acceptable?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Request SOW to check subcontracting clause | Day 1–2 |
| Request evidence of subcontracting (invoices, contract) | Day 3 |
| Request Client's approval (emails, conversations) | Day 3 |
| Assess quality of subcontracted work | Day 5–10 |
| Tier 1 AI assessment | Day 15 |
| **Tier 2 ADR Official determination** | **Day 45–90** |

### Evidence Required

**From Client:**
- SOW provision on subcontracting (if any)
- Expectation that Freelancer personally would do the work
- Any communications explicitly prohibiting subcontracting
- Proof of discovery of subcontracting (how did Client find out?)
- Evidence of lack of approval

**From Freelancer:**
- Evidence of subcontractor's role (invoice, contract, work samples)
- Claim of approval (email from Client saying "you can use a subcontractor" or "I know you work with a team")
- Argument that subcontracting is standard practice in the industry
- Quality evidence: Work meets acceptance criteria regardless of who did it

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **What does the SOW say about subcontracting?** — Check:
   - MSA Clause 15.11: "Freelancer may not engage subcontractors without prior written approval"
   - Does the SOW expressly authorize or prohibit subcontracting?
   - Or is it silent? (If silent, general principle applies: prohibition unless approved)

2. **Is the Freelancer's performance obligations personal?** — Some contexts require personal performance:
   - Consulting/advice role (Client needs Freelancer's expertise specifically)
   - Creative direction (design, writing — Client wants the Freelancer's voice)
   — Other contexts allow subcontracting (development, data entry)

3. **Was approval given?** — Check:
   - Explicit approval in email ("OK to use subcontractors")
   - Implicit approval (Client knew Freelancer works with contractors and didn't object)
   - Or no approval (Client prohibited it, or Freelancer never asked)

4. **Is the work actually good?** — This matters:
   - If subcontracted work meets acceptance criteria and Client is satisfied with the output, damage is minimal
   - If work is defective, subcontracting becomes a contributing factor

5. **What is Client's real concern?** — Distinguish:
   - Lack of transparency (Freelancer didn't disclose) — this is a breach of professional conduct
   - Quality issues (subcontracted work is inferior) — this is a performance breach
   - Economic harm (Client's downstream client sued, or loss of confidentiality) — this is a damage claim

### Possible Outcomes

**Subcontracting was Approved (Freelancer Compliant):**
- Client expressly or implicitly approved subcontracting
- No breach of Clause 15.11 (MSA)
- Work quality is acceptable
- Freelancer is compliant; Client must pay

**Subcontracting was Prohibited and Unauthorized (Freelancer in Breach):**
- SOW or Client communication expressly prohibited subcontracting
- Freelancer used subcontractor anyway
- Client is entitled to reject the work or demand replacement by Freelancer personally
- Client may seek damages (cost of rework, reputational harm)
- Payment held until re-performance by Freelancer

**Disclosure Breach (Professional Conduct Issue):**
- Freelancer was allowed to subcontract but failed to disclose
- Client didn't actually care about subcontracting, but feels deceived by lack of transparency
- Remedy: Apology, disclosure of subcontractor, quality assurance, possible fee reduction (professional conduct sanction)

**Subcontracting Conditional on Approval of Subcontractor:**
- SOW requires pre-approval of subcontractor's identity
- Freelancer engaged unapproved subcontractor
- Client may reject; Freelancer must redo with approved subcontractor
- Or Client accepts with fee reduction (professional conduct issue)

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Escalate if:**
- Relationship is ongoing and transparency issues need to be addressed through mediation
- Parties want to establish clear subcontracting guidelines for future engagements

## SCENARIO 8: CURRENCY / PAYMENT TIMING DISPUTE — INTERNATIONAL FREELANCER, GBP ESCROW, FX LOSSES CLAIMED

### Overview

**Common situation:** SOW specifies fees in GBP, but Freelancer is based overseas and wants payment in their local currency or USD. Freelancer claims Client should bear FX risk; Client claims Freelancer quoted in GBP and must accept GBP payment. If FX rates move, Freelancer claims loss.

### Trigger

- Engagement agreed: "£10,000" (in GBP)
- Freelancer expected to receive equivalent in USD/EUR (based on FX rate at time of agreement)
- By payment time, GBP has weakened; Freelancer receives less in home currency
- Freelancer claims Client should compensate for FX loss

### Triage

**Urgency:** Medium  
**Category:** Payment / Currency Risk  
**Risk Level:** Medium (FX risk allocation is a contract interpretation question)

**Initial Assessment:**
1. What does the SOW specify? (GBP? Reference rate?)
2. Was there an agreed FX rate or mechanism?
3. What is the FX loss amount?
4. Is the Freelancer based in a high-volatility currency jurisdiction?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Request SOW fee specification | Day 1 |
| Request Freelancer's bank details and currency received | Day 2 |
| Calculate FX loss (GBP at agreement date vs. payment date) | Day 5 |
| Assess FX risk allocation clause in MSA/SOW | Day 5 |
| Tier 1 AI assessment | Day 15 |
| **Tier 2 ADR determination** | **Day 30–60** |

### Evidence Required

**From Freelancer:**
- SOW fee specification (in what currency quoted?)
- Bank statement showing amount received (if different from quoted amount due to FX)
- Exchange rates: at SOW signature date vs. payment date
- Calculation of loss
- Argument that Client should bear FX risk

**From Client:**
- SOW fee specification (GBP)
- Evidence that fees were paid in full (GBP converted to foreign currency by Client's bank at market rate)
- Argument that FX risk was Freelancer's (Freelancer accepted GBP payment)
- Payment confirmation to Transpact

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **What does the SOW specify?** — Is the fee quoted in:
   - GBP only (Client bears no FX risk; Freelancer bears all FX risk between agreement and payment)
   - GBP with an "equivalent in USD at rate X" (parties have agreed on a FX rate; no later adjustment)
   - "GBP or USD equivalent at spot rate" (Client's rate, or Freelancer's bank rate?)

2. **Who has FX risk under the SOW?** — This is a contractual allocation:
   - If SOW says "GBP" only, Freelancer has FX risk (Freelancer should have hedged if concerned)
   - If SOW says "GBP equivalent in USD at rate [X]", fixed rate applies; no adjustment
   - If SOW is silent, default is: Client specifies currency (GBP); Client pays in GBP; Freelancer bears FX risk

3. **Is FX risk material?** — Depend on:
   - Time between agreement and payment (longer = more FX risk)
   - Volatility of the currency pair (GBP/USD volatility is moderate, GBP/TRY is high)
   - Amount of the fee (large fees = large FX swing)

4. **Did Freelancer hedge or mitigate?** — Could Freelancer have:
   - Requested interim payments to reduce FX exposure?
   - Purchased FX forwards to lock in rate?
   - Diversified Clients to reduce single-currency concentration?
   — Standard business practice is that contractor bears FX risk unless agreed otherwise

5. **What is fair?** — While law is clear (Freelancer bears FX risk if SOW specifies GBP), ADR Official may find:
   - FX movement was extreme (e.g., GBP dropped 30%) = Client may bear some loss
   - Movement was moderate (5–10%) = Freelancer bears all loss (standard)
   - Time between agreement and payment was unusually long = allocation may be fairer split

### Possible Outcomes

**Freelancer Bears All FX Loss (Standard Outcome):**
- SOW clearly specifies GBP
- FX risk was Freelancer's responsibility
- Client paid full GBP amount as agreed
- Freelancer's bank converted at the spot rate on payment date
- Freelancer bears loss

**Client Bears FX Loss (Rare — Extreme Circumstances):**
- FX movement was extreme and unforeseeable (e.g., >20% in short time)
- Freelancer had no reasonable way to hedge
- Fairness favors split or Client absorption

**Shared FX Loss (Compromise):**
- Movement was moderate (5–10%)
- Parties agree to share loss
- Example: Movement was £1,000 loss; split 50/50 → Client reimburses £500

**Fixed Rate Applied:**
- SOW specified "equivalent in USD at [rate X]"
- Client applies the fixed rate (even if spot rate is more favourable)
- No further adjustment based on actual FX rates

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Escalate if:**
- Parties want to negotiate a revised payment arrangement or FX hedge for future engagements
- Relationship is ongoing and parties want to establish a fairerfx risk allocation going forward

## SCENARIO 9: DATA BREACH DURING PROJECT — PERSONAL DATA COMPROMISED; GDPR OBLIGATIONS TRIGGERED

### Overview

**Common situation:** During the project, either Client or Freelancer experiences a data breach involving personal data (names, emails, customer lists, etc.). The other party demands to know who is liable and what remediation is required.

### Trigger

- Data breach discovered (data exfiltrated, server hacked, email account compromised, USB drive lost, etc.)
- Personal data was involved (GDPR breach, not just business data breach)
- One party blames the other
- Both parties concerned about regulatory obligations (notification, fines, etc.)

### Triage

**Urgency:** Critical  
**Category:** Data Protection / GDPR Breach  
**Risk Level:** Critical (regulatory, legal, reputational)

**Immediate Assessment:**
1. What personal data was breached?
2. Who was the "data controller" (responsible for the data)?
3. Who was the "data processor" (handled the data on behalf of controller)?
4. Was there a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) in place?
5. Whose security failure caused the breach?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Preserve evidence (do not destroy anything) | Day 1 |
| Notify ICO (Information Commissioner's Office) if serious | Day 1–3 |
| Clarify DPA status and data controller/processor roles | Day 2 |
| Investigate root cause of breach | Day 5–10 |
| **ADR determination may not be appropriate; refer to ICO** | **Day 30+** |

### Evidence Required

**From Both Parties:**
- Data Processing Agreement (DPA) (MSA Clause 17 requires it)
- Breached data: description, number of affected individuals, sensitivity
- Security measures in place (encryption, access controls, firewalls)
- Incident response: when was breach discovered, who reported it, what remediation
- Forensic evidence (if available): logs, incident report, timeline
- Data controller and processor roles (who is responsible under GDPR)

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **Is a Data Processing Agreement in place?** — MSA Clause 17:
   "If either Party processes personal data…the Party must enter into AllSquared's Data Processing Agreement (DPA) before any personal data is processed."

   If no DPA, that is itself a GDPR breach (both parties at fault for non-compliance).

2. **Who is the Data Controller?** — The party who determines "why" and "how" personal data is processed. Usually:
   - Client is Controller (Client decides to collect customer email addresses)
   - Freelancer is Processor (Freelancer handles emails on Client's behalf)

   If roles are unclear, both may be joint Controllers (shared liability).

3. **Whose security failure caused the breach?**
   - Client's security failure = Client liable (if Client is Controller)
   - Freelancer's security failure = Freelancer liable (but Client may also be liable for not vetting security)
   - Third-party vendor breach (Transpact, email provider) = may be third-party liability, not Client/Freelancer

4. **Was there reasonable security in place?** — GDPR Article 32 requires "appropriate technical and organizational measures":
   - Encryption (yes/no?)
   - Access controls (who could access the data?)
   - Incident response plan (did parties have one?)
   - Vendor vetting (was third-party vendor security-vetted?)

5. **What is the liability?**
   - Controller (Client) bears primary liability to affected individuals and ICO
   - Processor (Freelancer) bears liability for own security failures and indemnifies Controller if Processor caused breach
   - But ICO may find both at fault if DPA is missing

### Possible Outcomes

**Freelancer (Processor) Liable (Freelancer caused or failed to prevent breach):**
- Freelancer failed to implement security measures required by DPA
- Freelancer's system was hacked due to inadequate security
- Freelancer indemnifies Client for:
  - ICO fines
  - Third-party damages (individuals can sue)
  - Notification costs, credit monitoring, etc.
- Freelancer may be sued by ICO as well

**Client (Controller) Liable (Client's own failure or inadequate vetting):**
- Client failed to implement security controls on Freelancer's side
- Client did not adequately vet Freelancer's security practices
- Client is primarily liable to ICO and affected individuals
- Freelancer may have a limited indemnity (Freelancer provided reasonable security)

**Both Liable (Shared Responsibility):**
- No DPA in place (both should have executed one, both at fault)
- Both parties had security gaps (shared responsibility)
- Liability split based on relative fault

**Third-Party Vendor Liable (Data breach outside Client/Freelancer control):**
- Transpact's servers were hacked (Transpact liable, Client and Freelancer may seek indemnity from Transpact)
- Email provider's servers compromised (email provider liable)
- Client and Freelancer may both pursue vendor (not each other)

### Escalation Criteria

**Do NOT use ADR for GDPR breaches. Instead:**
- Notify ICO immediately (within 72 hours if serious)
- Parties should consult with Data Protection Officer (DPO) or DPA legal counsel
- Court proceedings may be necessary if damages are large
- ADR can assess contractual indemnity (who pays) but not GDPR compliance (regulatory)

### Template Communications

**On Data Breach Discovery:**

"A data breach has been discovered involving [DESCRIBE DATA]. This requires immediate action.

CRITICAL: Both parties must:
1. Preserve all evidence (servers, logs, emails) — do not delete
2. Conduct forensic investigation (hire external firm if needed)
3. Notify ICO if breach is serious (within 72 hours)
4. Notify affected individuals if required
5. Consult Data Protection Counsel

ADR proceedings are paused pending ICO investigation. Do not discuss breach details with third parties except with legal counsel and ICO.

Parties should report to ICO first; ADR can address liability and indemnity after investigation."

## SCENARIO 10: DISAGREEMENT ABOUT WHAT CONSTITUTES A "WORKING DAY" ACROSS DIFFERENT JURISDICTIONS

### Overview

**Common situation:** MSA and SOW reference "Working Days" (e.g., "Client must respond within 5 Working Days"). But Client is in UK (UK bank holidays), Freelancer is in Australia (different public holidays). Dispute arises about whether specific dates should be counted.

### Trigger

- Dispute Window or deadline involves "Working Days"
- Client is in UK, Freelancer is in Australia (different holidays)
- Deadline is 14 Working Days from delivery
- Freelancer claims deadline is sooner (doesn't count US holidays); Client claims deadline is later (counts AU holidays)
- Parties dispute whether a particular day is a "Working Day"

### Triage

**Urgency:** Medium (usually affects deadlines, not contract substantives)  
**Category:** Contract Interpretation / Timing  
**Risk Level:** Low-Medium (usually a procedural issue, not big impact)

**Initial Assessment:**
1. How many days are in dispute?
2. Is the deadline critical or can it be extended?
3. What is the MSA's definition of "Working Day"?

### Timeline

| Milestone | Days from Submission |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Check MSA Clause 1.1 definition of "Working Day" | Day 1 |
| Apply definition to disputed dates | Day 2 |
| Determine correct deadline | Day 3 |

### Evidence Required

**From Both Parties:**
- MSA definition of "Working Day" (Clause 1.1)
- Calendar: dates in dispute and holidays applicable in each jurisdiction
- SOW: which jurisdiction's holidays should apply?
- Any prior agreement on which holidays count

### Decision Framework (for ADR Official)

**Key Questions:**

1. **What does MSA Clause 1.1 say?** — MSA Template says:
   "Working Day" means Monday to Friday, excluding UK bank holidays and Christmas week (22 December to 2 January).

   The definition specifies "UK bank holidays" → UK holidays apply, not AU or other.

2. **Is the definition explicit or does it need interpretation?** — If MSA specifies "UK holidays", then AU holidays don't apply (Freelancer in AU must work on AU public holidays if they don't overlap with UK).

3. **Which calendar is the correct one?** — Since MSA specifies UK bank holidays, use UK calendar. Australian public holidays are irrelevant.

4. **Are there overlapping holidays?** — Some holidays (Christmas, New Year) overlap. Both parties recognize these and agree they are non-Working Days.

### Possible Outcomes

**MSA Definition Controls (Standard Outcome):**
- MSA defines "Working Day" as excluding "UK bank holidays"
- Use UK calendar only
- Apply definition mechanically to the disputed dates
- Deadline is X date (calculated using UK calendar)

**Amendment Negotiated (for Ongoing Engagement):**
- If parties disagree frequently about deadlines, parties may agree to amend MSA to specify:
  - "Use UK bank holidays only, regardless of Freelancer's jurisdiction"
  - Or: "Use either UK or Freelancer's jurisdiction holidays, whichever is more favourable to Freelancer"
  - Or: "Deadlines are in calendar days, not Working Days"

**Extension Granted (Equitable Remedy):**
- If the dispute has caused delay and neither party is entirely at fault, ADR Official may recommend extending the deadline by 3–5 Working Days
- Non-binding recommendation (both parties can agree)

### Escalation Criteria to CEDR

**Not suitable for CEDR.** This is a pure contract interpretation question with a mechanical answer once the definition is understood.
