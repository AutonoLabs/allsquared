## 1. CTSI PRE-SUBMISSION ENGAGEMENT STRATEGY

### 1.1 Why a pre-application call is essential

CTSI has no published guidance specifically addressing AI-augmented ADR platforms. AllSquared will be among the first of its kind to apply. Submitting cold — without prior engagement — risks:
- a return of the application for additional information (adding 4–8 weeks);
- a request for a formal undertaking on AI oversight that could constrain product design;
- informal rejection without written reasons if CTSI has concerns about the AI model.

**A pre-application call is strongly recommended and is standard practice** for novel applicants. CTSI's ADR approval team is relatively small and approachable.

### 1.2 How to initiate

1.2.1 **Contact CTSI ADR approval team** via their commercial services division. Email: [adr@tradingstandards.uk — confirm current address on tradingstandards.uk]. Request a pre-application discussion for a "novel AI-assisted ADR platform."

1.2.2 **Frame the call correctly.** Do not lead with "AI dispute resolution." Lead with: *"We are applying as a CTSI-approved ADR entity for consumer disputes. Our platform uses technology-assisted case management with human ADR Official oversight. We'd like to discuss our application structure before submission."* This is accurate and positions AllSquared as a compliant operator, not a disruptor seeking regulatory workarounds.

1.2.3 **Agenda for the call:**
- Confirm CTSI is the appropriate competent authority for AllSquared's dispute sectors
- Confirm scope exclusions (FCA-regulated, energy, legal services)
- Discuss how CTSI assesses AI-assisted ADR — specifically the "ADR official" definition and human oversight requirements
- Clarify what Schedule 2 documentation CTSI currently requires for technology platforms
- Confirm CTSI's current application processing timeframe

1.2.4 **Who attends:** Eli (as the named ADR Official / MCIArb) + legal adviser. Keep it focused. Eli's MCIArb credential is the headline — lead with it.

### 1.3 What to have ready before the call

- Draft Procedural Rules (prepared ✅)
- Draft Conflict of Interest Policy (prepared ✅)
- One-page summary of AllSquared's AI oversight framework (human ADR official → AI tool → human signs off)
- Eli's MCIArb certificate

### 1.4 Timing

Initiate CTSI contact within **2 weeks**. Allow 2–3 weeks for the call to be scheduled. Application submission at week 6–7 of the 8-week target is realistic.

## 2. FINANCIAL SERVICES DISPUTES — SCOPE DECISION

### 2.1 The regulatory fork

Financial services disputes (e.g., insurance, banking, consumer credit, investments) fall under **FCA jurisdiction** as competent authority under Schedule 1 Part 1 of the ADR Regulations. The mandatory ADR body for FCA-regulated firms is the **Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS)**.

This means:
- AllSquared **cannot** position itself as the approved ADR entity for disputes involving FCA-regulated traders — FOS has that statutory role.
- However, AllSquared **can** handle disputes *about* services adjacent to financial services where the trader is not itself FCA-regulated (e.g., a fintech offering a non-regulated product).

### 2.2 Recommendation: Exclude FS disputes at launch — hard boundary

**Do not include financial services disputes in the initial CTSI application.** Reasons:

2.2.1 Including FS disputes would trigger a parallel FCA track — a separate, more complex approval process with FCA as competent authority, higher fees (reg 15A), and significantly longer timeline (FCA approval can take 6+ months).

2.2.2 FCA-regulated firms are legally mandated to offer FOS — AllSquared cannot displace that obligation. Including FS disputes in the application without FCA approval creates regulatory risk.

2.2.3 CTSI will be more comfortable approving AllSquared quickly if the scope is clearly non-FS. A clean, scoped application moves faster.

### 2.3 Phase 2 — FS disputes

If AllSquared wants to handle FS-adjacent disputes at scale, the correct Phase 2 structure is:

- Apply to FCA as a competent authority OR
- Partner with FOS as a technology layer (possible but requires FOS cooperation) OR
- Restrict FS-adjacent work to non-regulated disputes only (e.g., disputes about a SaaS platform used by an FCA-regulated firm, where the dispute is about the SaaS contract — not the regulated service itself)

Phase 2 timeline: 6–9 months post-CTSI approval. Recommend scoping this separately after launch.
