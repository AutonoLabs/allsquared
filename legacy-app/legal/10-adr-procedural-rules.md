## 1. ABOUT THESE RULES

1.1 These Rules govern how disputes are resolved through AllSquared's Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) scheme.

1.2 **Who we are:** AllSquared Limited is an approved ADR entity under the Alternative Dispute Resolution for Consumer Disputes (Competent Authorities and Information) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/542), approved by the Chartered Trading Standards Institute (CTSI).

1.3 **What these rules cover:** These Rules apply to all disputes arising from engagements facilitated through the AllSquared Platform (https://allsquared.io), where the parties have agreed to use AllSquared's ADR service.

1.4 **Plain English:** These Rules are written in plain English to help you understand your rights and how the process works. If you need clarification, contact us at [disputes@allsquared.io].

---

## 2. SCOPE OF COMPLAINTS

2.1 **Types of disputes covered:** AllSquared's ADR scheme covers disputes between a Client and a Freelancer relating to:

    (a) non-delivery or partial delivery of a Deliverable;
    (b) defective work or failure to meet acceptance criteria;
    (c) disputes about scope of work (in-scope vs. out-of-scope);
    (d) payment disputes (refusal to pay, overpayment, incorrect deductions);
    (e) intellectual property ownership or licensing disputes;
    (f) breach of confidentiality or misuse of materials;
    (g) termination of engagement and payment for work to date;
    (h) any other breach of the AllSquared Master Services Agreement (MSA) or Statement of Work (SOW).

2.2 **Disputes excluded from scope:**

    (a) Claims exceeding £25,000 (unless both parties agree in writing to submit to ADR);
    (b) disputes relating to regulated financial products (e.g., investment products, insurance, mortgages) — handled by FOS or other designated regulator;
    (c) employment disputes (disputes with employees, not freelancers);
    (d) disputes relating to AllSquared's operation of the Platform (complaints about Platform outages, bugs, or service failures);
    (e) disputes already determined by a court, arbitrator, or other ADR entity;
    (f) disputes relating to criminal conduct (reported to law enforcement instead);
    (g) disputes that are frivolous, vexatious, or manifestly unfounded.

2.3 **Consumers vs. B2B:** These Rules primarily apply to consumer disputes (where one party is a consumer and the other a business). B2B disputes (both parties are businesses) may also be resolved through AllSquared's ADR scheme, but only if both parties have agreed in writing to submit to ADR before the dispute arose.

## 3. WHO CAN COMPLAIN

3.1 **Eligible Complainants:**

    (a) Consumers (any person acting for purposes outside their trade, business, or profession);
    (b) B2B parties (both Client and Freelancer are businesses) — only if both have agreed in writing to ADR in the MSA or SOW.

3.2 **Traders:** For purposes of these Rules, a "trader" is a person or entity acting in a business capacity (the Freelancer providing services or the Client purchasing services). If a party is a consumer, AllSquared's ADR scheme applies automatically. If both parties are traders, ADR applies only by agreement.

3.3 **Representation:** Any party may be represented by a lawyer, accountant, or other representative in ADR proceedings. Representation is at the representing party's own cost.

## 4. TIME LIMITS FOR COMPLAINTS

4.1 **Limitation Period:** A dispute must be submitted to AllSquared's ADR scheme within 12 months of:

    (a) the date of delivery of the final Deliverable (if the dispute is about delivery or quality);
    (b) the date on which payment was due (if the dispute is about payment);
    (c) the date of the alleged breach (for other disputes);
    (d) whichever is latest.

4.2 **Post-Rejection Clock:** If a Client rejects a Deliverable during the Dispute Window (clause 5 of the MSA), the Client may initiate ADR proceedings up to the end of the Dispute Window. After Auto-Release occurs (14 Working Days), the Client must still submit a dispute within 12 months of the original delivery date to be in time.

4.3 **Internal Process First:** Before submitting to ADR, the complaining party must first attempt to resolve the dispute directly with the other party (good faith negotiation) for at least 10 Working Days. Disputes submitted to ADR without this attempt may be refused on grounds of failure to exhaust internal remedies (see clause 8 below).

## 6. REQUIRED INFORMATION IN COMPLAINT

6.1 **Complaint Form:** The online portal guides you through a complaint form. You must provide:

    (a) **Complainant Details:** Your name, contact email, phone number, business name (if applicable);
    (b) **Respondent Details:** The other party's name and Platform profile ID;
    (c) **Project Reference:** The SOW number or Project ID on AllSquared;
    (d) **Nature of Dispute:** Clear description of what went wrong (500 words maximum);
    (e) **Outcome Sought:** What you are asking for (refund, rework, payment, etc.);
    (f) **Evidence:** Attach relevant documents — SOW, chat messages, Deliverables, acceptance screenshots, payment evidence, photos, etc.;
    (g) **Negotiations Undertaken:** Brief description of how you have tried to resolve the matter directly.

6.2 **Incomplete Complaints:** If your complaint is incomplete, AllSquared requests clarification within 5 Working Days. If you do not provide clarification within 10 Working Days, the complaint may be refused on grounds of insufficient information.

6.3 **Evidence Standards:** You must provide evidence to support your claim. Mere assertion, without supporting documents, is insufficient. If the complaint relies on oral evidence or contradicts the other party's account, both parties will have the opportunity to explain during Tier 2 proceedings.

## 7. THE THREE-TIER DISPUTE RESOLUTION PROCESS

**TIER 1: AI-ASSISTED FACILITATION**

7.1 **What Tier 1 is:** Tier 1 is not formal ADR. It is an automated, AI-powered analysis designed to facilitate a negotiated settlement without human intervention or cost.

7.2 **How it works:** Upon receipt of a valid complaint, AllSquared's AI system:

    (a) analyzes the complaint, the SOW, and the MSA;
    (b) reviews evidence provided by the complaining party;
    (c) generates a proposed resolution (e.g., partial refund, rework, full payment, split liability);
    (d) presents the proposal to both parties with a recommendation.

7.3 **Acceptance or Rejection:** Either party may accept the AI proposal (resulting in settlement) or reject it. Rejection does not require grounds or justification. If either party rejects the proposal, the matter proceeds automatically to Tier 2.

7.4 **No Fee:** Tier 1 is free of charge to both parties.

7.5 **Not Binding:** Tier 1 proposals are not binding determinations. A party may reject a proposal without penalty.

7.6 **Timeline:** Tier 1 is completed within 10 Working Days of the complaint being submitted. If resolution is reached, settlement is documented and funds released via Escrow. If rejected, the matter escalates to Tier 2.

**TIER 2: ADR OFFICIAL DETERMINATION**

7.7 **What Tier 2 is:** Tier 2 is formal Alternative Dispute Resolution conducted by an independent ADR Official appointed by AllSquared. Tier 2 is regulated under SI 2015/542.

7.8 **Binding vs. Non-Binding:** The ADR Official's determination is:

    (a) **Binding** if both parties agreed in writing before submitting to Tier 2 that the determination is binding; or
    (b) **Non-Binding** (a formal opinion) if the parties did not agree in advance that it is binding.

Whether binding or non-binding, both parties must hear the determination and are given the right to appeal to court if non-binding.

7.9 **The Process:**

    (a) AllSquared assigns an ADR Official with no conflicts of interest (see Conflict of Interest Policy);
    (b) both parties are notified and provided with the ADR Official's declaration of independence;
    (c) both parties have 10 Working Days to submit evidence, written submissions, and requests for a hearing;
    (d) the ADR Official reviews all submissions and the Tier 1 AI analysis;
    (e) if either party requests a hearing, the ADR Official conducts a hearing (telephone/video conference) within the 90-day period;
    (f) the ADR Official issues a written determination, explaining the reasoning and legal basis.

7.10 **The 90-Day Clock:**

    (a) the clock starts from the date AllSquared confirms it has received a complete file from both parties (both parties have submitted all evidence and are ready for determination);
    (b) the clock is paused if either party requests additional time (up to 10 Working Days);
    (c) the clock is paused during any requested hearing;
    (d) the determination must be issued within 90 calendar days or as soon as reasonably practicable thereafter;
    (e) if the 90-day deadline is exceeded, the complaining party may apply to CTSI (see clause 15).

7.11 **Outcome Types:**

    (a) **Recommended Resolution (Non-Binding):** The ADR Official recommends a settlement (e.g., refund, rework, payment adjustment) but does not enforce it;
    (b) **Binding Determination:** If parties agreed in advance, the determination is binding on both parties and is treated as a binding contract term;
    (c) **Expert Opinion:** The ADR Official provides a detailed opinion on the merits but stops short of a determination if the legal position is uncertain.

**TIER 3: CEDR-ACCREDITED MEDIATION**

7.12 **What Tier 3 is:** If both parties agree after Tier 2 determination, the dispute may be escalated to CEDR-accredited mediation for a final attempt at settlement by a neutral mediator.

7.13 **Voluntary:** Tier 3 is entirely voluntary and requires written agreement from both parties. It is not automatic and is not mandatory ADR.

7.14 **Costs:** CEDR fees apply, charged according to CEDR's published fee schedule at https://www.cedr.com. Costs are typically split equally between parties unless otherwise agreed.

7.15 **Timeline:** Tier 3 mediation is typically completed within 30–60 days of both parties agreeing.

## 8. GROUNDS FOR REFUSING A COMPLAINT

8.1 AllSquared may refuse to accept or to continue with a complaint if:

    (a) **Vexatious:** The complaint is manifestly abusive or made with the intention of harassing the other party;
    (b) **Frivolous:** The complaint has no reasonable prospect of success or is trivial;
    (c) **De Minimis:** The complaint is below £25 in value;
    (d) **Time-Barred:** The complaint is submitted more than 12 months after the events giving rise to the dispute;
    (e) **Not Within Scope:** The subject matter is not covered by these Rules (e.g., employment dispute, regulated financial product);
    (f) **Already Determined:** The dispute has already been determined by a court, arbitrator, or other ADR entity;
    (g) **Insufficient Information:** The complainant has not provided sufficient evidence or information after being requested to do so;
    (h) **No Internal Resolution Attempt:** The complaining party has not attempted good faith negotiation for at least 10 Working Days before submitting to ADR;
    (i) **Anonymous Complaint:** The complainant has not provided their name and contact details.

8.2 **Decision to Refuse:** Refusal is decided by AllSquared's Dispute Manager in consultation with the ADR Official (if appointed). Written notice of refusal is given to the complainant within 5 Working Days, explaining the reason and the right to appeal the refusal to CTSI.

8.3 **Appeal to CTSI:** If a complaint is refused, the complainant may appeal the refusal to the Chartered Trading Standards Institute (CTSI) within 10 Working Days. CTSI may overturn the refusal or uphold it.

## 9. THE 90-DAY CLOCK

9.1 **When It Starts:** The 90-day clock starts from the date both parties have submitted all evidence and AllSquared's Dispute Manager has confirmed to both parties that a "complete file" has been received.

9.2 **What Pauses It:**

    (a) either party requesting a hearing with the ADR Official (the clock is paused during the hearing period);
    (b) either party requesting up to 10 Working Days to provide additional evidence;
    (c) any force majeure event or significant delay caused by circumstances beyond AllSquared's control.

9.3 **What Happens If Exceeded:** If the 90-day period is exceeded without good reason:

    (a) the ADR Official must issue the determination as soon as possible;
    (b) the complaining party may lodge a complaint to CTSI about the delay;
    (c) any delay does not invalidate the determination, but CTSI may investigate and require AllSquared to make improvements.

9.4 **Measurement:** The 90 days are calendar days, not working days, and include weekends and bank holidays.

## 10. OUTCOME TYPES AND LEGAL EFFECT

10.1 **Binding Determination (if parties agreed in advance):**

    (a) a binding determination is enforceable as a contract term;
    (b) either party may seek court enforcement of a binding determination if the other party does not comply;
    (c) a binding determination can be appealed to court only on grounds of procedural unfairness or lack of jurisdiction, not on the merits;
    (d) enforceability is not affected by whether the complaining party is satisfied with the outcome.

10.2 **Non-Binding Determination or Opinion:**

    (a) a non-binding determination is not enforceable by AllSquared;
    (b) either party may choose to accept or reject it;
    (c) if a party rejects it, the matter may proceed to court litigation;
    (d) a non-binding determination may be cited as evidence in subsequent litigation but is not binding on the court.

10.3 **Recommended Settlement (Tier 1):**

    (a) recommended settlements are not binding unless the party explicitly accepts them;
    (b) if both parties accept, the settlement is documented and funds are released;
    (c) if either party rejects, the matter escalates to Tier 2.

## 11. COSTS

11.1 **Consumer Disputes — Free ADR:**

    (a) if the complainant is a consumer, Tier 1 (AI Facilitation) and Tier 2 (ADR Official Determination) are free;
    (b) AllSquared bears the cost of ADR as part of its business model.

11.2 **B2B Disputes — Fee Schedule:**

    (a) if both parties are businesses, ADR fees apply as set out in AllSquared's Fee Schedule at https://allsquared.io/adr-fees;
    (b) typical fees are: Tier 1 (Free), Tier 2 (£250–£500 depending on claim value);
    (c) fees are split equally between parties unless otherwise agreed or determined by the ADR Official;
    (d) the complaining party may be required to pay the B2B fee upfront before Tier 2 proceeds; if the complainant cannot pay, the complaint may be refused.

11.3 **Tier 3 (CEDR) Costs:**

    (a) Tier 3 costs are borne by the parties in accordance with CEDR's Fee Schedule;
    (b) costs are typically split equally unless the mediator directs otherwise.

11.4 **Representation Costs:**

    (a) if a party is represented by a lawyer or other professional, the cost is borne by that party and is not reimbursable by the other party, unless the ADR Official determines otherwise on grounds of bad faith conduct.

## 12. RIGHT TO WITHDRAW

12.1 Either party may withdraw from ADR proceedings at any time before the ADR Official's determination is issued, by giving written notice to AllSquared.

12.2 **Effect of Withdrawal:**

    (a) if the Freelancer withdraws, all Escrow funds held are returned to the Client (minus any agreed portion);
    (b) if the Client withdraws, Escrow funds are released to the Freelancer in accordance with the current terms;
    (c) withdrawal does not prevent the other party from pursuing the matter in court litigation.

12.3 **Withdrawal During Hearing:** If a party withdraws during or immediately after an ADR Official hearing, the ADR Official may still issue a determination based on evidence to date, unless both parties agree to abandon the matter entirely.

---

## 13. LEGAL EFFECT AND ENFORCEMENT

13.1 **Binding Determination — Enforcement:**

    (a) a binding determination has the legal effect of a contract term;
    (b) either party may enforce a binding determination through the courts;
    (c) an unsatisfied party may apply to the High Court for summary judgment or specific performance;
    (d) enforcement costs are recoverable if enforcement is successful.

13.2 **Non-Binding Determination — Effect:**

    (a) a non-binding determination is evidence of the ADR Official's view but is not binding;
    (b) either party may rely on it as evidence in subsequent court litigation;
    (c) the court is not bound by the determination but may give it evidential weight;
    (d) cost of pursuing litigation after rejection of a non-binding determination is the pursuing party's responsibility.

13.3 **Settlement Reached in Tier 1 or Tier 3:**

    (a) if both parties agree to a settlement, the settlement agreement is documented in writing;
    (b) the settlement is binding on both parties as a contract term;
    (c) Escrow is released in accordance with the settlement terms;
    (d) either party may enforce the settlement through court enforcement if the other party does not comply.

## 14. YOUR RIGHTS AS A PARTY

14.1 **Right to Independent Advice:** You have the right to seek independent legal, financial, or professional advice before accepting an ADR determination. AllSquared can provide contact details for free legal advice services.

14.2 **Right to Representation:** You may be represented by a lawyer, accountant, union representative, or other professional of your choosing. Representation is optional and at your cost.

14.3 **Right to Hearing:** You have the right to request a hearing with the ADR Official before a determination is issued. Hearings are by telephone, video conference, or (if both parties agree) in person.

14.4 **Right to Object to ADR Official:** If you believe the ADR Official assigned to your dispute has a conflict of interest or lacks impartiality, you may object in writing within 10 Working Days of being notified of the Official's appointment. AllSquared will investigate and, if your objection is valid, replace the Official.

14.5 **Right to Withdraw:** You may withdraw from ADR at any time before determination is issued.

14.6 **Right to Court:** ADR proceedings do not prevent you from pursuing litigation. You may commence court proceedings at any time, even while ADR is ongoing. Commencement of court proceedings does not automatically terminate ADR proceedings, but AllSquared may stay (pause) ADR proceedings pending court determination if appropriate.

14.7 **Right to Information:** You have the right to request information about:

    (a) progress of your dispute;
    (b) the ADR Official's background, qualifications, and experience;
    (c) the conflict of interest declarations made by the ADR Official;
    (d) the reasons for any decision to refuse or pause your complaint.

## 15. COMPLAINTS ABOUT ALLSQUARED'S ADR SERVICE

15.1 **What Can Be Complained About:**

    (a) breach of these Procedural Rules (e.g., missed 90-day deadline, bias, lack of impartiality);
    (b) breach of the Conflict of Interest Policy;
    (c) failure to provide fair process or hearing;
    (d) discriminatory treatment.

15.2 **How to Complain:**

    (a) first, raise the complaint directly with AllSquared's Dispute Manager at [disputes@allsquared.io];
    (b) AllSquared will investigate and respond within 10 Working Days;
    (c) if you are not satisfied with AllSquared's response, you may escalate to CTSI.

15.3 **Escalation to CTSI:**

AllSquared's competent authority is the Chartered Trading Standards Institute (CTSI). You may file a complaint about AllSquared's ADR service directly with CTSI:

**Chartered Trading Standards Institute**
Website: https://www.tradingstandards.uk
Email: [ADR complaints contact — to be confirmed]
Postal address: [CTSI ADDRESS]

15.4 **CTSI's Powers:** CTSI may:

    (a) investigate the complaint;
    (b) direct AllSquared to remedy a breach;
    (c) impose sanctions on AllSquared for serious or repeated breaches;
    (d) withdraw AllSquared's approval as an ADR entity if breaches are severe or systemic.

15.5 **Complaints to CTSI do not affect:**

    (a) the validity of an ADR determination;
    (b) your right to appeal a determination to court (on grounds of procedural unfairness);
    (c) your right to pursue litigation.

## 16. PRIVACY AND CONFIDENTIALITY

16.1 **Confidentiality of Proceedings:** All submissions, communications, and evidence provided in ADR proceedings are confidential and "without prejudice" (i.e., inadmissible in subsequent litigation except by agreement).

16.2 **Publication of Determinations:** ADR Official determinations are published on AllSquared's website on a anonymised basis (with parties' names redacted) to ensure transparency and consistency in ADR decisions. You may opt out of publication by requesting confidentiality in your complaint form.

16.3 **Data Protection:** AllSquared processes personal data in connection with ADR proceedings in accordance with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. Your data is used only for the purpose of resolving your dispute and is not shared with third parties except where required by law.
